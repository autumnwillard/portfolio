More clients wasn't the fix. Better-fit clients were.

## The problem

We built websites and brands for small local businesses. Over time, those clients got flaky. ==About three of them paid late or stopped paying altogether,== whether that was invoices or their hosting fees. Three doesn't sound like many. For a startup our size, it was a lot, and the whole business felt unstable.

<InkArrow from="problem" to="cause" seed={7} />

## Digging in

The obvious answer was "find more clients." But more small clients meant more of the same risk, plus more work to find them. So I looked at the math instead.

Five clients who can each pay $400 for a website bring in about what one client paying $1,500–$2,000 does. The five are far more work, though. Each one is another chance of not getting paid, and you have to keep hunting for the next batch.

The bigger realization: ==a small client wasn't a small job.== Every project still needed the brand discussion, rounds of iteration, an outline of the site and then the build. A $400 website took about as much of that work as a bigger one did.

<StickyNote tilt={2} color="yellow" size="sm">
Quantity is a great strategy for jelly beans. Less so for invoices.
</StickyNote>

## Constraints

- **We still needed clients who needed us.** Go too big and the company has its own IT team. They don't need a small studio to build their website.
- **We were tiny.** I was the only product and design person, so any new direction had to fit the team we already had.

<InkArrow from="cause" to="fix" seed={12} flip />

## What I did

I pitched a pivot to the founder: go after bigger fish, but only the ones that still need what we do. That sweet spot was **mid-size businesses**:

- more stable, so they're less likely to stop paying
- able to pay more per project
- still full of website, brand and IT pain points, with no dedicated internal team to handle them

<figure>
  <TechHandoff2x2 />
</figure>

**The argument:** we were already doing the full amount of work for every client. If the brand talks, the iteration, the outlining and the building were going to happen either way, we should be doing them for clients who could pay for it.

**Carrying the strategy into the work.** "Quality over quantity" showed up in client work too. I repositioned a custom-leatherwork client from small, low-cost goods to premium bespoke work, then carried that through its logo, print ads and website prototype. A snow-plow client asked for a brand guide, and I pitched truck magnets on top of it. They went with the magnets. I also designed and ran print magazine and Facebook ad campaigns.

## What happened

==The company adopted the pivot and still targets this segment.==

## Looking back

The complaint sounded like a sales problem. It was a fit problem. Now when someone says "we need more," I ask "more of what, exactly?"
